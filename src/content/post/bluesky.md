I asked [Claude](https://claude.ai/) to help me formulate a social media strategy for Bluesky. Here's what we came up with together. I'm not 100% sold on the strategy, but I thought there were some interesting ideas I wouldn't come up with on my own.

## My Objectives

1. Connect with people interested in data science, causal inference, programming, and data visualization
2. Minimize time spent on platform (15 minutes daily)
3. Leverage my interests: data science, causal inference, econometrics, statistics, programming, data visualization

## Core Strategy: "The Causal Data Scientist"

**Positioning**: Bridge the gap between rigorous statistical thinking and practical data applications. My generalist nature becomes a superpower - I see patterns others miss because I'm not siloed in pure ML.

**Key insight**: The causal inference/econometrics crowd is less saturated than general ML/AI content, has higher quality discussions, and often includes decision-makers (economists, policy researchers, senior data scientists).

## Content Pillars (3-4 posts/week rotation)

1. **"Causal Finds"** - Interesting studies/papers with my statistical take
2. **"Stats in the Wild"** - Real-world examples where causal thinking matters  
3. **"Method Mondays"** - Explain econometric concepts for practitioners
4. **"Quasi-Experiments"** - Natural experiments I spot in data

## 15-Minute Daily Workflow

- **5 min**: Engage authentically on 3-5 posts from causal inference/econometrics accounts
- **7 min**: Create/schedule content 
- **3 min**: Respond to comments on my posts

## Tactical Approach

**Hashtags**: #CausalInference #Econometrics #Statistics #DataScience #QuasiExperiments

**Timing**: Post when data scientists are active (9-11 AM EST weekdays)

**Content Style**: Lead with insight/takeaway, ask questions to encourage engagement

**Target Engagement**: Focus on accounts discussing A/B testing methodology, observational studies, policy evaluation, academic economists, statistics educators

## Quick Wins

- Repurpose blog content (e.g., clustered standard errors → "Why your A/B test results might be wrong")
- Comment thoughtfully on accounts with 500-5K followers  
- Join existing causal inference conversations

## Market Dynamics & Opportunity

**Why ML Dominates Demand:**
- Immediate ROI perception ("predict customer churn" vs "test causality")
- Tangible outputs (models, APIs) vs abstract knowledge
- Tech industry influence and hype cycles
- Easier for managers to understand

**The Strategic Opportunity:**
Companies are graduating from "build anything that works" to "build things we understand and can improve systematically." They're realizing they need both prediction AND causation.

**My niche value**: Speaking both languages - bridging predictive ML and causal inference for organizations ready to move beyond correlation.
